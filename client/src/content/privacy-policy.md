## 1. About this Policy

This Privacy Policy explains how **[INSERT LEGAL ENTITY NAME] ABN [INSERT ABN] trading as Silara Marketing** (**Silara**, **we**, **us** or **our**) collects, holds, uses and discloses personal information in connection with:

- the Silara Marketing website, enquiries, demonstrations and business development;
- our business-to-business products and services, including the **APGP Referral Program, IncidentIQ, NoteGuard, CredsVault and ProviderPulse**;
- customer onboarding, account administration, support, billing, security and service improvement; and
- our dealings with customer representatives, authorised users, suppliers, professional advisers and other business contacts.

Silara supplies services to organisations and authorised business users. Our products are not directed to participants, residents, patients, clients or consumers for personal use. A customer may nevertheless choose to process information about those individuals through an approved product workflow. In that situation, the customer remains responsible for its collection notices, consents, legal authority, records, care decisions and regulatory obligations.

We intend to handle personal information consistently with the **Privacy Act 1988 (Cth)** and the Australian Privacy Principles (**APPs**) where they apply. The APPs address the personal-information lifecycle, including transparent management, collection, notice, use and disclosure, direct marketing, overseas disclosure, security, access and correction.[1] Health information is sensitive information and attracts stricter requirements; the category can include personal information collected while providing disability, aged-care or allied-health services.[2]

## 3. Personal information we may collect

Depending on the interaction, we may collect the following categories.

### 3.1 Business-contact and identity information

This may include a person's name, role, organisation, business email address, business telephone number, work location, professional profile, preferred contact method and authority to act for an organisation.

### 3.2 Account and security information

This may include account identifiers, authentication records, role and permission settings, login activity, audit logs, security events and records of account administration. We do not ask users to disclose passwords to support personnel.

### 3.3 Commercial and service information

This may include product interests, proposals, Order Forms, subscription or service details, invoices, payment status, support requests, meeting notes, implementation records, customer feedback and correspondence.

### 3.4 Website, device and usage information

This may include IP address, browser and device type, approximate location derived from an IP address, referring pages, pages viewed, interactions, diagnostic information and cookie or similar-technology identifiers. Our current cookie and analytics practices are described in our Cookie Notice.

### 3.5 Customer Content

**Customer Content** means information submitted, stored, generated or managed by a customer or its authorised users through an approved product workflow. Depending on the product and customer configuration, Customer Content may include:

- vacancy, property, service-capability and referral information for APGP;
- incident workflow, evidence, action, deadline and approval information for IncidentIQ;
- draft documentation, review comments and quality-rule results for NoteGuard;
- worker identity, screening, qualification, registration, training and expiry information for CredsVault; and
- feedback, response, service-recovery and reputation-workflow information for ProviderPulse.

Some Customer Content may be sensitive information, including health information, disability information, incident information or professional and employment information. Customers must not upload sensitive information unless the relevant product and written agreement permit it and the customer has a lawful, necessary and proportionate reason to do so.

### 3.6 Information we do not need in marketing forms

Do not submit participant, resident, patient, client, incident, clinical, worker-screening, credential-document or other sensitive information through our general contact, demonstration, newsletter, quiz or marketing forms. If we receive unnecessary sensitive information, we may securely delete it, restrict access to it or ask the sender to use an approved secure channel.

## 4. How we collect personal information

We may collect information:

- directly from an individual through forms, email, meetings, calls, demonstrations and product use;
- from a customer's administrators or other authorised users;
- from referral partners, suppliers, professional advisers or service providers;
- from public business sources where lawful and reasonable; and
- automatically through website, product, security and diagnostic technologies.

Where practicable, we collect personal information from the individual concerned. If a customer supplies information about another person, the customer must have authority to do so and must provide any required privacy notice or obtain any required consent.

## 5. Why we collect, use and disclose information

We may handle personal information to:

- respond to enquiries, arrange calls, provide demonstrations and assess product fit;
- create and administer accounts, verify authority and manage permissions;
- provide, configure, support, secure, maintain and improve contracted services;
- process APGP vacancy and referral activity under the applicable written agreement;
- communicate service, security, support and operational notices;
- invoice customers, administer payments and maintain business records;
- investigate misuse, protect people and systems, and manage legal or security incidents;
- analyse service performance and improve usability, reliability and customer support;
- meet legal, regulatory, insurance, audit and dispute-management requirements;
- manage suppliers, advisers, corporate transactions and business operations; and
- send optional business marketing where permitted, with a clear way to opt out.

We will not use Customer Content to make independent care, clinical, safeguarding, suitability, employment, regulatory or participant-facing decisions. We will not use Customer Content for unrelated advertising. Any use of Customer Content to improve a product beyond providing the contracted service must be authorised by the applicable agreement, lawfully permitted, and subject to appropriate safeguards such as aggregation or effective de-identification.

## 6. Sensitive and health information

Health information is sensitive information under Australian privacy law. The OAIC describes valid consent as informed, voluntary, current and specific, given by a person with capacity.[2] Customers are responsible for determining when consent or another legal authority is required before collecting or disclosing sensitive information to Silara.

Customers using Silara products must apply data minimisation, role-based access, purpose limitation and their own record-management rules. De-identification involves more than removing a name; re-identification risk must be actively assessed in context.[2] Silara does not promise that information is de-identified merely because direct identifiers have been removed.

## 13. Direct marketing

We may send relevant B2B marketing where we have the required consent or another permitted basis. Commercial electronic messages must accurately identify the sender, include correct contact details and provide a clear unsubscribe facility. ## 16. Data breaches

We maintain a process for assessing and responding to suspected data breaches. Under the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme, a covered entity must assess suspected eligible data breaches and notify the OAIC and affected individuals where the statutory serious-harm test is met and remedial action has not removed the likelihood of serious harm.[4]

Where an incident affects Customer Content, Silara and the customer will cooperate in accordance with their agreement and applicable law. The organisation with the relevant legal notification responsibility retains responsibility for the final assessment and notification, although the parties may coordinate communications.

## 17. Product and care-sector boundaries

Silara products support business workflows; they do not transfer a provider's statutory, clinical, care, employment or safeguarding responsibility to Silara.

| Product | Privacy and responsibility boundary |
|---|---|
| APGP | Providers remain responsible for participant authority, vacancy accuracy, suitability assessment, safeguarding, service agreements and permitted referral disclosures. General vacancy enquiries must not include unnecessary sensitive participant information. |
| IncidentIQ | Providers remain responsible for immediate safety action, incident classification, investigation, preservation of evidence, reporting deadlines, notifications and authorised closure decisions. |
| NoteGuard | Providers remain responsible for lawful records, factual accuracy, clinical or professional judgement, original-record preservation and final human approval. |
| CredsVault | Providers remain responsible for lawful worker-data collection, identity and credential verification, screening, employment decisions, access permissions and retention. |
| ProviderPulse | Providers remain responsible for lawful feedback requests, privacy-aware responses, service recovery, platform rules and avoiding review gating, suppression or misleading practices. |

Registered NDIS providers must maintain incident-management systems and notify reportable incidents within applicable timeframes; use of IncidentIQ does not itself satisfy or replace those obligations.[5] Aged-care providers likewise retain responsibility for rights-based care, governance, quality systems, incident management, complaints and protected information under the applicable aged-care framework.[6] [7]
